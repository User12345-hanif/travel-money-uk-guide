Travel Money Tips for UK Travellers

Spending money abroad is rarely just about the exchange rate. ATM charges, card fees, and being offered to pay in pounds instead of the local currency can quietly add up. A little planning before you travel usually saves more than hunting for a slightly better rate on the day.

Five tips that actually help

  1. Check ATM fees before you go. Some ATMs charge a fixed fee on top of your bank's own charge, and some do not. Estimating that cost in advance helps you decide whether to take out larger, less frequent withdrawals or to rely more on card payments.
  2. Pay in the local currency, not in pounds, when a card machine or ATM offers the choice. This choice is called dynamic currency conversion. The rate offered at the till is often worse than your card provider's rate, even when the screen makes the pounds figure look convenient.
  3. Plan with a budget, not a guess. Work out roughly how much you expect to spend each day on food, transport, and activities, then add a buffer for the unexpected. A trip planner helps turn that daily figure into a total to prepare for.
  4. Carry a mix, not only cash or only a card. A card is useful for hotels and larger purchases, while some cash is still handy for small shops, markets, and transport. Keeping the two separate also means one lost item does not leave you with nothing.
  5. Check what your bank or card provider actually charges. Foreign transaction fees, ATM withdrawal fees, and weekend markups vary widely. The figure that matters is the total cost of getting and spending the money, not the headline exchange rate alone.
